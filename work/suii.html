Mobile app concept · 2022 · Rental service

SUII, a suitcase rental app

A rental service for expensive, rarely used travel gear. Interviews and surveys, one persona, wireframes, and final screens for browsing, booking dates and tracking a delivery.

Role
Strategy, user interviews, competitive review, persona, user flow, wireframes, final design
Timeline
2022
Tools
Figma for wireframes and design, Photoshop for image work, Illustrator for icons and the logo
Process
Research, analyze, ideate, design, test
Links
Full board on Behance

The idea

Travelers buy expensive things they use once: a hard-shell suitcase, a carry-on with a battery, a duffel for one trip. The purchase is a burden and so is the storage afterwards. SUII is a rental service that makes those items easy to get for a few days and easy to give back.

Research

I ran interviews and a survey around four questions: whether people had bought an expensive item for a single trip, how the effort of acquiring those items affected their travel, whether they would rent instead, and what would make a rental service worth using. The answers pointed at two things, storage and cost, more than at the products themselves.

The problem statement: develop a rental service that streamlines access to expensive, seldom-used items for travelers, simplifying the process and reducing the cognitive load of acquiring them.

Decisions

Wireframes before visuals

I wireframed the main screens first to get feedback on the flow before any styling, which caught the biggest flaw early: the booking calendar had been buried two taps deep.

Onboarding that gets out of the way

A splash screen, a login with three sign-in options, and you are on the store. Nothing to configure before you can look.

Browse like a store, read like a spec sheet

The home page filters by suitcases, bags, organizers and duffels and shows what is available near you. A product page reads like a spec sheet: weight, capacity, colors, and the one detail that matters for a traveler, such as a built-in battery.

Dates first, then a map

Booking asks for the trip dates and pickup and drop times on one screen. After that the app is a tracking map: your suitcase is on the way, arriving in 17 minutes.

What I would do next

Test the booking screen with five travelers and watch where they hesitate. Then decide whether pickup and return should be delivered or collected, which the concept leaves open.
